The word "resonance" has had a strange career. In physics it means something specific: an oscillating system amplifies its response to an external driver whose frequency matches its own natural frequency. That is measurable, and a great deal rests on it, from bridges brought down by resonance and a tuned radio receiver to MRI, which is built on nuclear magnetic resonance.

In esoterica "resonance" serves as a *synonym for "connection"* without specifying anything: "resonance with the universe", "resonance with another person", "raise your vibration". Such phrases are not necessarily false; they simply carry no information, because they do not say what is oscillating or at what frequency.

What interests me is the middle ground, the place where physics overlaps with what people call states of consciousness.

## What definitely exists: neural synchronisation

The brain generates electrical oscillations that can be picked up from the scalp with EEG. By frequency they are usually divided like this:

— **delta** (0.5–4 Hz): deep sleep;
— **theta** (4–8 Hz): drowsiness, meditation, learning;
— **alpha** (8–13 Hz): relaxed wakefulness;
— **beta** (13–30 Hz): active thinking;
— **gamma** (30–100 Hz): attention, binding perception together.

These rhythms are produced by ensembles of neurons firing in sync. Synchronisation is a physiological process: it can be shifted (by meditation, stimulation, drugs) and measured.

When two people interact, whether talking, touching or doing something together, *interpersonal synchronisation* appears: the rhythms of their brains begin to align. This is measured in experiments that record two brains at once, known as hyperscanning (Dumas et al., *PLoS ONE*, 2010; Dikker et al., *Current Biology*, 2017).

When people say "we're on the same wavelength", they are unknowingly describing a specific phenomenon: the alignment of rhythms and the timing of neural ensembles across two separate brains.

## What possibly exists: resonance with the environment

Schumann resonances are standing electromagnetic waves in the cavity between the Earth's surface and the ionosphere. The fundamental is about 7.83 Hz, with overtones near 14, 21 and 27 Hz. This is measured and undisputed.

Does the human brain resonate with these frequencies? Some researchers (König, Cherry, Persinger) say yes. Most of that work is methodologically weak, with small samples and no preregistration. In the serious literature the question remains open.

The same goes for geomagnetic fields. There are statistical correlations between geomagnetic activity and human behaviour (aggression, anxiety, strokes). The effects are small but measurable (Palmer et al., 2006), and the mechanism is unknown.

None of this counts as proof. It is an example of a grey zone: conscientious scientists work there with testable hypotheses, but there is not yet enough evidence either way.

## Where resonance definitely *fails*

"Resonating with another person's intention through walls, at a distance." This has been tested many times, under controlled conditions and with preregistration, and no effect has been found. Dean Radin of the Institute of Noetic Sciences claims otherwise, but his meta-analyses have been criticised on methodological grounds (Hyman, 2010), and they do not survive independent replication.

"Resonance with crystals, stones, pyramids", in the sense of drawing energy or information from them, is physically meaningless. A crystal does not emit electromagnetic oscillations of any significant power on its own; the quartz in a watch oscillates only because an electrical circuit drives it.

## What to take away

When someone says "resonance", ask: at what frequency, between which systems, and how is it measured?

If the answer is "two brains synchronising in conversation", that is a physical fact you can study. If it is "the brain and the Earth through Schumann frequencies", that is an open question that calls for caution. "Resonance with cosmic consciousness" steps outside the physical meaning of the word, and all that is left is "a connection I feel but cannot point to".

That last one is not necessarily false. But a physics term derails the conversation here. Better to find another word and finish the thought.
